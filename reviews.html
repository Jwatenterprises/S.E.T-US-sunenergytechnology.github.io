What Matters in Each Scenario

How we judge generators for four common situations, using manufacturer specs cross-checked against third-party test data. Runtimes below are estimates — plug your own loads into our solar calculator.

🔌

24-Hour Power Outage

Keeping a fridge, phones, a laptop and LED lights running through a full day.

  • Usable capacity: plan on about 85% of the rated Wh after inverter losses. A 2,000Wh unit gives roughly 1,700Wh of real AC power.
  • Fridge math: a typical full-size fridge averages 100–150W over a day (about 2.4–3.6kWh), so a 2kWh unit covers essentials for roughly 12–18 hours before recharging.
  • Surge rating: compressors pull 3–5× their running watts for a split second at startup. Check the surge spec, not just continuous output.
  • UPS / pass-through: a fast switchover (under ~30 ms) keeps routers and CPAP machines from rebooting when the grid drops.
⛺

Multi-Day Camping

Portability and solar recharge matter more than raw capacity.

  • Weight: under about 30 lbs is a one-hand carry; 50–65 lbs units need two people or a cart.
  • Solar input: the max solar watts decide how fast you refill. Expect real panel output around 70–85% of the panel rating.
  • Recharge estimate: usable Wh ÷ (panel watts × 0.8) ≈ hours of good sun to refill.
❄️

Cold Weather

Batteries lose capacity in the cold, and charging below freezing can damage them.

  • LiFePO4 (LFP): long cycle life, but most cells must not be charged below 32°F (0°C). Look for a stated low-temperature charge limit or built-in self-heating.
  • Expect less capacity: output drops noticeably in freezing temperatures; keep the unit insulated or indoors and bring it up to temperature before charging.
  • Check the spec sheet: both the discharge and the charge temperature ranges, not just one.
🔊

Noise

Fan noise matters when sleeping, working, or camping near others.

  • Fans scale with load: most units are near-silent at light loads and loudest when charging fast or running near max output.
  • Slow charging is quieter: many apps let you cap the AC charge rate overnight.
  • Use published dB ratings where the manufacturer lists them, and compare them at the same load level.

Solar Generator FAQ

How much does the Jackery Explorer 2000 Plus weigh?

About 61.5 lbs (27.9 kg). It has built-in wheels and a telescoping handle, so you roll it rather than carry it. For comparison, the EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max weighs 50 lbs and the Anker SOLIX C1000 about 27.6 lbs.

Can you connect two Jackery power stations together?

Not by plugging one into the other. Never connect two power stations' AC outputs together. Jackery's expandable models add capacity with matching battery packs: the Explorer 2000 Plus takes up to five Battery Pack 2000 Plus for about 12kWh, and Jackery lists the 2000 Plus system at up to 24kWh when two units are used together with Jackery's own connection hardware. Check Jackery's compatibility list for your model before buying.

Can the battery in a solar generator be replaced?

Generally not by the owner. The internal battery is serviced by the manufacturer, usually under warranty. Expansion batteries add capacity but don't replace the internal pack. LiFePO4 models are rated for thousands of cycles (the Jackery 2000 Plus is rated for 4,000 cycles to 70% capacity), so the battery typically lasts many years of regular use.

Is a Jackery or EcoFlow good for an RV?

Yes, for running appliances and charging devices. To plug the whole RV in, check the shore-power plug: many RVs use a 30A (TT-30) connector, so confirm the power station has a matching outlet or use a properly rated adapter. Keep loads in mind too: a 2kWh unit runs lights, a fridge, and fans well, but a rooftop AC drains it in a few hours.

What size solar generator do I need?

Add up the watts of what you want to run and how many hours you'll run each item. As a rough guide, plan on about 85% of a unit's rated Wh being usable. Our free solar calculator does the math for you.
