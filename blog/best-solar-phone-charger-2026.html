How We Tested

Marketing wattage is fiction. A panel rated at 28W in a lab almost never delivers 28W on a trail, at a campsite, or on your dashboard. We tested every charger with a calibrated USB power meter in three real-world conditions:

  • Full direct sun — panel perpendicular to sunlight, no shade, midday
  • Partly cloudy — intermittent cloud cover, typical camping weather
  • Partial shade — under tree canopy with dappled sunlight

We charged an iPhone 15 Pro and a Samsung Galaxy S24 with each panel and recorded actual watts delivered, time to full charge, and how each panel handled transitions between sun and shade (some panels reset their charging negotiation when clouds pass, which kills output for 30–60 seconds each time).

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a solar charger fully charge a phone?

Yes. A 28W panel like the BigBlue charges an iPhone 15 from 0 to 100% in about 2–3 hours in direct sunlight. A 40W panel like the FlexSolar does it in under 2 hours. Cloudy conditions roughly double the charge time. The key is sustained direct sunlight — partial shade dramatically cuts output.

Solar charger vs. solar power bank — which is better?

A foldable solar panel (like the ones on this list) delivers 10–40W of real power and charges phones in hours. A solar power bank has a tiny 1–5W panel that takes 20–40 hours of sunlight to charge itself — the solar panel is essentially decorative. For reliable solar charging, buy a dedicated panel and a separate power bank. The BioLite SolarPanel 10+ bridges both with its built-in 3,000mAh battery.

How many watts do I need?

For one phone: 10W minimum, 20–28W recommended. For two phones and a power bank: 28–40W. For a phone plus a portable power station: 100W+. More wattage also means better performance in cloudy or partly shaded conditions, where lower-wattage panels may not produce enough output to trigger charging at all.

Do solar chargers work on cloudy days?

Yes, but output drops significantly. In light cloud cover, expect 50–60% of rated output. In heavy overcast, expect 10–25%. A 28W panel in heavy clouds might only deliver 3–7W — enough to slowly charge a phone but not fast charge it. This is where higher-wattage panels shine: a 40W panel in clouds still produces more usable power than a 10W panel in partial sun.

Can I use a solar charger to charge a laptop?

Not directly from the small panels on this list. Laptops need 45–100W from a USB-C PD port. The Anker 625 (100W) can charge some USB-C laptops directly via its USB-C port, but most 20–40W panels don't deliver enough power. For laptop charging, pair a solar panel with a portable power station that has an AC outlet or high-wattage USB-C PD output.
