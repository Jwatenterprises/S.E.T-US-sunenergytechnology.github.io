How to Calculate Your Total Needs

  1. List every appliance in your truck that runs on electricity.
  2. Add up the running watts of everything that runs simultaneously during peak service. This is your minimum continuous output requirement.
  3. Check the highest single startup surge — usually your refrigerator or freezer compressor. Your generator’s surge rating must exceed this.
  4. Multiply your average total draw by your hours of operation to get total Wh needed. Add 20% buffer for inefficiency.
Example: BBQ food truck. Refrigerator (300W) + exhaust fan (200W) + LED lights (100W) + POS system (75W) + phones (40W) = 715W continuous draw. Over 8 hours: 5,720 Wh + 20% buffer = ~6,864 Wh needed. The griddle and smoker run on propane (most BBQ trucks don’t use electric griddles). A BLUETTI AC200L (2,400W output, 2,048Wh) plus one expansion battery (total 4,096Wh) handles this all day with solar panel top-up.

Section 179 Tax Deduction: Write Off the Full Purchase Price

A solar generator purchased for business use qualifies as a Section 179 deduction under the IRS code. This allows you to deduct the full purchase price of business equipment in the year you buy it, rather than depreciating it over several years.

What this means in practice:

  • A $3,999 EcoFlow DELTA Pro Ultra could save you $880–$1,480 in federal taxes (at the 22–37% bracket), effectively reducing your net cost to $2,519–$3,119.
  • Solar panels purchased for business use are also deductible under Section 179.
  • Expansion batteries count as additional equipment — deductible.
  • The deduction applies to sole proprietors (Schedule C), LLCs, S-Corps, and C-Corps.
💰 Pro tip: Buy your solar generator before December 31 to claim the deduction for this tax year. Keep your receipt and document the business purpose (e.g., “primary power source for [Your Business Name] food truck operations”). Consult your CPA or tax professional for your specific situation — we’re not tax advisors.

Adding Solar Panels to Your Food Truck

A solar generator without solar panels is just a battery on wheels. Adding panels to your truck roof turns it into a self-recharging power system that extends runtime by 30–50% during service hours.

How Many Panels Do You Need?

  • 2x 200W panels (400W total): Delivers ~280–340W in real conditions. Adds approximately 2,200–2,700 Wh over 8 hours of sun. Fits on most food truck roofs. Best for supplementary charging while parked.
  • 4x 200W panels (800W total): Delivers ~560–680W. Adds approximately 4,500–5,400 Wh over 8 hours. Covers a large food truck roof. Can nearly match the daily draw of a light food operation.
  • Rigid vs. flexible panels: Rigid panels are more efficient and cheaper per watt. Flexible panels conform to curved truck roofs and add almost no weight. For permanent truck roof installations, flexible panels are usually the better choice.

Most food truck operators start with 2 panels ($400–$600) and add more if they need longer runtime. The panels mount semi-permanently to the truck roof using adhesive or bolt-on brackets. For panel recommendations, see our best solar panels guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a solar generator power a food truck?

Yes. Modern LiFePO4 solar generators deliver 2,400–7,200W of continuous output, which is enough for most food truck setups. The EcoFlow DELTA Pro Ultra (7,200W) can power an entire commercial kitchen. Mid-range units like the BLUETTI AC300 (3,000W) handle refrigeration, blenders, POS systems, and lighting simultaneously. The key is matching your total wattage draw — including compressor startup surges — to the generator’s output and surge ratings.

How much does it cost to run a food truck on solar vs gas?

A gas generator costs food truck operators $600–$1,200/year in fuel (2–4 gallons/day × 200 operating days at $3.50–$4.50/gallon) plus $200–$400/year in maintenance. Over 5 years, total gas generator costs run $6,600–$15,500 including equipment replacement. A solar generator costs $1,499–$3,999 once with $0 ongoing fuel or maintenance costs, and lasts 10+ years. The payback period is typically 2–4 years. See our full cost comparison.

What size solar generator do I need for a food truck?

It depends on your equipment. A basic coffee/smoothie truck needs 1,500–2,500W. A full-service truck with an electric griddle needs 3,000–5,000W. For battery capacity, multiply your average draw by your operating hours: a 2,000W average draw over 8 hours = 16,000Wh. Use our wattage calculator table above to add up your specific appliances. Start with the continuous output requirement (what runs simultaneously), then check the surge rating for compressor startups.

Can I write off a solar generator on my taxes?

Yes. A solar generator used for business qualifies as a Section 179 deduction, allowing you to deduct the full purchase price in the year you buy it. A $3,999 solar generator could save $880–$1,480 in federal taxes depending on your bracket. Solar panels and expansion batteries are also deductible. This applies to sole proprietors, LLCs, and corporations. Keep your receipt and document the business purpose. Consult your tax professional for your specific situation.

Do solar generators work in winter or cloudy weather?

Yes. The battery stores energy regardless of weather — charge it from a wall outlet overnight (1–3 hours for most units) and you’re ready for the next day. Solar panels are supplementary daytime charging, not your only power source. In winter or heavy clouds, solar panel output drops 30–60%, but most food truck operators charge fully at home and use panels to extend runtime during service. LiFePO4 batteries operate down to -4°F, so cold weather performance isn’t a concern.
