The 320Ah Self-Heating solves the biggest problem with budget LiFePO4 batteries: cold-weather charging. LiFePO4 batteries cannot be safely charged below 32°F — doing so causes permanent lithium plating on the anode, reducing capacity and creating safety risks. Dakota Lithium avoids this by rating discharge to −20°F (but still limits charging to 32°F). LiTime’s answer: build the heater directly into the battery.

When the internal temperature drops below 41°F (5°C), the self-heating element activates automatically using the battery’s own stored energy. It warms the cells to safe charging temperature before allowing current to flow in. No external heating pad needed. No manual intervention. The BMS handles everything.

At 320Ah / 4,096Wh, this is also LiTime’s highest-capacity single-battery option. With Bluetooth 5.0 for real-time monitoring, it’s the complete package for cold-climate solar, RV, and marine installations. How to Build a LiTime Solar Battery Bank

Building a battery bank with LiTime is straightforward. Here’s the step-by-step:

  1. Size your needs. Calculate daily watt-hour consumption. A typical RV or small cabin uses 2,000–4,000Wh/day. A home emergency backup runs 3,000–6,000Wh/day for essentials (fridge, lights, devices, router).
  2. Choose battery count. Divide daily consumption by 1,024Wh (80% usable from a 100Ah battery). Two 100Ah batteries = 2,560Wh total / 2,048Wh usable. One 300Ah battery = 3,840Wh / 3,072Wh usable.
  3. Solar panels. BougeRV 200W portable or rigid panels. Rule: panel watts × peak sun hours (4–6) = daily recharge. 400W × 5 hours = 2,000Wh daily recharge.
  4. MPPT charge controller. BougeRV Sunflow 30A ($89) or Victron SmartSolar. Must be set to LiFePO4 profile (14.6V absorption, 13.6V float). Never use lead-acid settings on lithium batteries.
  5. Wiring. Parallel connections (same model, same age): 10AWG minimum for runs under 20ft. Ring terminals from controller to battery. Use a fused bus bar for 3+ batteries.
  6. Inverter (optional). Pure sine wave inverter converts 12V DC to 110V AC. Size to your maximum simultaneous load. 2,000W covers most household essentials.

Budget example: Two LiTime 12V 100Ah ($671.98) + BougeRV 200W panel ($249) + BougeRV Sunflow 30A ($89) + 2,000W inverter (~$150) = $1,159.98 for a complete 2,560Wh solar battery system. The same system with Dakota Lithium batteries would cost $1,987 — $827 more.

How long do LiTime batteries last?

4,000+ full cycles to 80% capacity (6,000+ for the 200Ah Xtra Mini). At daily cycling, that’s 10.9–16.4 years before the battery drops below 80% original capacity. Can I connect multiple LiTime batteries together?

Yes. Parallel for same voltage + combined capacity (two 12V 100Ah = 12V 200Ah / 2,560Wh). Series for higher voltage (two 12V = 24V). LiTime supports up to 4 batteries in parallel and 4 in series (4P4S = 48V 400Ah / 20,480Wh). Always use the same model, capacity, and age. Built-in BMS supports both configurations.
