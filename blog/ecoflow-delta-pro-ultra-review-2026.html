Where the DELTA Pro Ultra Wins

  • Accessibility. You can buy it right now on Amazon, have it delivered this week, and plug it in yourself. Tesla Powerwall requires finding a certified installer, scheduling an in-home assessment, waiting 3-6 months, and paying $9,200+ for installation. For many homeowners, the installer bottleneck is the real dealbreaker.
  • LiFePO4 chemistry. LiFePO4 offers superior thermal stability, fire resistance, and longer cycle life compared to Tesla's NMC chemistry. 3,000+ cycles to 80% is excellent, and LiFePO4 continues to degrade gracefully beyond that threshold.
  • Expandability at scale. 90kWh from a single inverter vs. Tesla's 54kWh max (4 units). For large homes, rural properties, or serious off-grid setups, the DELTA Pro Ultra scales further.
  • Cost per kWh at scale. A 2-battery DELTA Pro Ultra (12.2kWh) costs ~$6,098. Two Tesla Powerwalls (27kWh) cost $18,400+ installed. Per kWh: ~$500/kWh for EcoFlow vs. ~$681/kWh for Tesla.
  • Portability option. The DELTA Pro Ultra can be moved. During evacuations, you can load it into a vehicle. A Powerwall is bolted to your wall.

Where Tesla Powerwall Wins

  • Seamless grid integration. Powerwall integrates directly into your home's electrical system with an automatic transfer switch included in the installation price. The DELTA Pro Ultra requires an optional Smart Home Panel 2 (~$1,599 + electrician) for equivalent integration.
  • Higher base capacity. 13.5kWh per unit vs. 6.1kWh. Out of the box, a single Powerwall holds more energy.
  • 10-year warranty vs. EcoFlow's 5 years. Tesla also includes performance guarantees for daily solar self-consumption cycling.
  • Integrated solar inverter. Powerwall 3 includes a built-in solar inverter, simplifying rooftop solar installations. The DELTA Pro Ultra requires a separate solar setup.
  • Round-trip efficiency. Tesla's power electronics are optimized for daily solar self-consumption with high round-trip efficiency. The DELTA Pro Ultra is primarily designed for backup, not daily cycling against time-of-use rates.
The bottom line: If you have an existing solar installation, plan to do daily self-consumption cycling, and don't mind the 3-6 month waitlist, Tesla Powerwall is still the gold standard for grid-integrated home batteries. But if you want backup power now, don't have or don't want a solar installer, value the ability to expand to 90kWh, or want something you can physically move — the DELTA Pro Ultra is the clear winner. Most of our readers fall into the second camp.

Two Ways to Set It Up

The DELTA Pro Ultra works in two distinct modes. Most buyers start with Option A and upgrade to Option B later.

Option A: Plug-and-Play Backup (No Electrician)

Out of the box, the DELTA Pro Ultra works as a manual backup system. You charge it from any standard wall outlet (up to 3,200W), and when the power goes out, you plug your critical appliances directly into the inverter. Refrigerator, CPAP machine, Wi-Fi router, phone chargers, a few lights — all run directly from the unit.

This setup costs $3,999, requires no permits, no electrician, and no modifications to your home's electrical panel. You can have it running the day it arrives. This is how most of our readers use it.

Option B: Whole-Home Integration (Smart Home Panel 2)

For automatic whole-home switchover, EcoFlow offers the Smart Home Panel 2 (~$1,599, sold separately). A licensed electrician installs it between your main breaker panel and your circuits, allowing the DELTA Pro Ultra to automatically detect an outage and take over your selected circuits within milliseconds — the same behavior as a Tesla Powerwall.

With the Smart Home Panel 2, the total system cost for 6.1kWh of automatic whole-home backup is ~$5,598 ($3,999 + $1,599) plus electrician labor — still significantly less than a Tesla Powerwall installation. And you can start with plug-in backup today and upgrade to the Smart Home Panel whenever you're ready.

Solar Pairing — 5,600W of Direct Input

The DELTA Pro Ultra accepts up to 5,600W of solar input through its High PV and Low PV ports. That's the highest solar input of any direct-buy home battery system on the market. In practical terms:

  • 4 × 400W rigid panels (1,600W) recharges the 6.1kWh battery in about 4 hours of peak sun.
  • 8 × 400W panels (3,200W) cuts that to under 2 hours.
  • Full 5,600W array recharges from zero in about 1.1 hours — fast enough to keep the system fully charged through heavy daytime use during extended outages.

The combined charging rate of 8,800W (AC + Solar) means you can charge from both the grid and solar panels simultaneously, filling the battery at extraordinary speed when both sources are available. For off-grid setups or properties with frequent outages, the solar throughput is a serious differentiator. See our best solar panels guide for panel recommendations.
