How We Ranked the Jackery Lineup

Jackery was one of the first brands to put portable solar generators on the map, and in 2026 their lineup has completed a full transition to LiFePO4 battery chemistry with the "Plus" series. The result is four models that share the same design language and battery tech but serve genuinely different buyers — from a sub-$300 camping companion to a 12,000Wh expandable home battery system.

We didn't rank these four models on spec sheets alone. Jackery's numbers are straightforward, but the question is which numbers matter for how you'll actually use the unit. We weighed five factors:

  • Value. What you get in capacity and output per dollar spent, and whether the price reflects genuine capability or just brand recognition.
  • Performance. Real continuous output, surge handling for compressor-driven appliances, and how fast each unit recharges from solar and from the wall.
  • Expandability. Whether you can grow the system later without replacing it outright, and what that expansion actually costs.
  • Battery chemistry & longevity. Every model here uses LiFePO4, but cycle life varies — the 1000 Plus and 2000 Plus get 4,000 cycles, while the 300 Plus and 600 Plus get 3,000.
  • Real-world use. Not "can it technically power X" but "will it comfortably power X for as long as you actually need it."

The result is a ranking built around fit, not just horsepower. The Explorer 2000 Plus has the biggest numbers on this list, but it's not automatically the "best" Jackery — it's the best Jackery if you're backing up your entire house or running multiple heavy appliances simultaneously. For most people reading this, the Explorer 1000 Plus hits the sweet spot of capacity, portability, price, and expandability. Below, we rank all four by where each one genuinely earns its price.

Step up from the 1000 Plus when your needs cross from "keep the essentials alive" into "back up most of the house." The Explorer 2000 Plus is Jackery's heavy hitter — 3,000W of continuous output (6,000W surge) handles window AC units, power tools, and multiple heavy appliances running simultaneously without tripping. That's the threshold where you go from rationing loads to running your home close to normally during an outage.

What separates the 2000 Plus from everything else in Jackery's lineup is raw expansion capacity. With five add-on battery packs, the system scales to 12,000Wh — genuine home battery territory. At that scale, you're looking at multi-day whole-home coverage in most climates, or indefinite coverage if you pair it with a serious solar panel array. The 1,400W solar input means a multi-panel setup can recharge the base unit in a couple of hours of strong sun, and combined with the expansion batteries, the system can sustain itself indefinitely during extended grid outages.

The trade-off is straightforward: at $1,999 and 49.2 lbs, the 2000 Plus costs nearly double the 1000 Plus and weighs 18 lbs more. If you don't routinely need to run a window AC and a microwave at the same time, or if you don't plan to expand past 5,000Wh, the 1000 Plus does the job for less money and less weight. The 2000 Plus earns its price when your load list genuinely demands 3,000W and your backup window needs to stretch past a day.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Jackery solar generator should I buy in 2026?

It depends on your use case. For camping, van life, and keeping small electronics charged, the Explorer 300 Plus ($279) is the lightest and cheapest LiFePO4 option at just 7.74 lbs. For home backup essentials (fridge, lights, router, CPAP) and RV power, the Explorer 1000 Plus ($1,099) delivers the best balance of capacity, output, expandability, and price with 1,264Wh and 2,000W output. For whole-home backup with multiple heavy appliances, the Explorer 2000 Plus ($1,999) offers 3,000W output and expands to 12,000Wh. For mid-range needs like tailgating and light home backup, the Explorer 600 Plus ($549) fills the gap with 632Wh and 800W output. Take our 2-minute quiz for a personalized recommendation.

Are all Jackery Explorer Plus models LiFePO4?

Yes. Every model in Jackery's current Explorer Plus lineup uses LiFePO4 (lithium iron phosphate) battery chemistry. The Explorer 1000 Plus and 2000 Plus are rated for 4,000 charge cycles to 80% capacity, while the Explorer 300 Plus and 600 Plus are rated for 3,000 cycles. LiFePO4 is a significant upgrade over the older NMC lithium-ion cells used in Jackery's original Explorer line, offering longer lifespan, better thermal stability, and improved safety for daily use and hot climates.

Can I expand the Jackery Explorer 1000 Plus capacity?

Yes. The Explorer 1000 Plus supports up to three additional battery packs, expanding from 1,264Wh to a maximum of 5,056Wh. The Explorer 2000 Plus supports up to five add-on packs for a maximum of 12,000Wh. The smaller Explorer 300 Plus and 600 Plus are not expandable. If you think you might need more capacity down the road, the 1000 Plus or 2000 Plus gives you room to grow without replacing the entire unit. Use our sizing guide to figure out how much capacity your loads actually need.

How does Jackery compare to EcoFlow and BLUETTI?

Jackery's Explorer Plus lineup is competitive with EcoFlow and BLUETTI on specs and price. The Explorer 1000 Plus offers similar capacity to the EcoFlow DELTA 2 at a comparable price point but with higher expandability. The Explorer 2000 Plus matches up well against the EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max and BLUETTI AC200L in capacity and output. Jackery tends to be more streamlined and user-friendly, while EcoFlow wins on AC charging speed and BLUETTI offers more modular configurations. All three brands now use LiFePO4 chemistry across their lineups. See our full BLUETTI vs Jackery comparison for the detailed breakdown.

How much solar input can each Jackery Explorer Plus accept?

Solar input scales with each model: the Explorer 300 Plus accepts up to 100W, the Explorer 600 Plus accepts up to 200W, the Explorer 1000 Plus accepts up to 800W, and the Explorer 2000 Plus accepts up to 1,400W. Higher solar input means faster off-grid recharging. The 1000 Plus and 2000 Plus can pair with Jackery's SolarSaga panels for multi-panel setups that recharge in a few hours of strong sun, while the 300 Plus and 600 Plus are best matched with a single folding panel.
