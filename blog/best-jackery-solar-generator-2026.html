Best Jackery Solar Generator 2026:
Full Lineup Ranked

We re-ranked Jackery’s lineup around what’s actually in stock on Jackery.com this fall — from the 5.5 lb Explorer 300D to the 12kWh-expandable HomePower 2000 Plus v2 — with current prices and specs.

By S.E.T. Editorial Team Published August 3, 2026 Updated October 1, 2026 🕑 14 min read

Quick Answer: The best Jackery solar generator for most people is the Explorer 1000 Plus ($619), with 1,264Wh of LiFePO4 capacity, 2,000W output, and 800W solar input. For expandable home backup, the HomePower 2000 Plus v2 ($1,069) starts at 2,048Wh and grows to 12kWh. For camping and electronics, the Explorer 300D ($209) weighs 5.5 lbs but has no AC outlets. Prices are from Jackery.com as of October 1, 2026.

How We Ranked the Jackery Lineup

Jackery’s U.S. lineup changed a lot in 2026. The Explorer 300 Plus and 600 Plus are no longer sold as standalone units, the Explorer 2000 Plus is down to kits and refurbished stock, and newer models — the Explorer 300D, the v2 Explorers, and the HomePower series — have taken their place. This guide ranks only models you can buy new on Jackery.com today.

We ranked on published specs and current pricing, weighing five factors:

  • Value. Capacity and output per dollar at today’s Jackery.com prices.
  • Performance. Continuous output, surge handling for compressor appliances, and recharge speed from AC and solar.
  • Expandability. Whether you can grow the system with add-on batteries that are still sold.
  • Battery life. Every model here is LiFePO4, rated from 4,000 to 6,000+ cycles.
  • Fit. Which buyer each model actually serves — a 5.5 lb DC-only pack and a 41 lb home battery are not competing for the same person.

800W Solar Input

Price: ~$619 (reg. $699) • Expandable: No (add-on packs discontinued) • Cycle life: 4,000 cycles

The Explorer 1000 Plus is the Jackery we'd point most buyers to first. It pairs 1,264Wh of LiFePO4 capacity with 2,000W of continuous output (4,000W surge) — enough to run a full-size refrigerator, lights, a router, laptops, and a CPAP together during an outage, with enough surge headroom for compressor startup.

Price is the big story this fall. Jackery.com lists it at $619 (regular $699), which puts a 1.2kWh, 2,000W station in the same price band as smaller 1kWh units. With 800W of solar input across two DC ports, a pair of large panels can refill it in a few hours of strong sun.

One change buyers should know: Jackery has discontinued the Battery Pack 1000 Plus, so you should treat this as a fixed 1,264Wh station rather than an expandable system. If you want room to grow, step up to the HomePower 2000 Plus v2 below.

Price: ~$1,069 (reg. $1,199) • Expandable: Up to 12kWh • Cycle life: 6,000 cycles

The HomePower 2000 Plus v2 is Jackery's current expandable home backup unit and the replacement for the Explorer 2000 Plus in our ranking (the standalone 2000 Plus is no longer sold new on Jackery.com). It starts at 2,048Wh with 2,400W of output and expands to 12kWh with add-on batteries — Jackery says that's enough runtime to ride out 2+ day outages.

Jackery's Priority Routing feature lets you set which circuits stay powered and automatically sheds non-essential loads at a battery threshold you choose. The LFP battery is rated for 6,000+ cycles, the highest in this lineup, and solar input peaks at 800W through two DC ports (per third-party testing).

At $1,069 on Jackery.com (regular $1,199), it costs more than the Explorer 2000 v2 for similar starting capacity — what you're paying for is the expansion path and the longer cycle life.

The Explorer 300D replaces the Explorer 300 Plus as our budget and camping pick — the 300 Plus is no longer sold as a standalone unit on Jackery's U.S. store. At 5.5 lbs, the 300D is light enough for a daypack and still carries 288Wh of LiFePO4 capacity.

Important: the “D” means DC. The 300D has no AC wall outlets — it delivers up to 300W through USB-C, USB-A, and DC ports, including a built-in woven 140W USB-C cable that fast-charges laptops and cameras. Jackery rates it for about 11 iPhone charges or roughly 10 hours of a Starlink Mini.

If you need to plug in anything with a standard plug (a mini-fridge, a fan, a CPAP without a DC cable), skip the 300D and look at the Explorer 1000 v2. For phones, laptops, lights, drones, and Starlink, it's the best-value Jackery at $209 (regular $249).

The Explorer 1000 v2 is the lighter, simpler 1kWh Jackery. You get 1,070Wh of capacity and 1,500W of output (3,000W surge) in a 23.8 lb unit — enough for a fridge, CPAP, coffee maker, or small microwave, plus a router and laptops through an evening outage.

Jackery's Emergency Super Charge takes it from 0–100% in about an hour on AC, and two DC inputs accept up to 400W of solar. Jackery adds two warranty years (five total) when you buy direct.

Compared with the 1000 Plus, you give up 194Wh, 500W of output, and half the solar input to save about $60 and 8 lbs. If weight matters more than raw power, the 1000 v2 is the better carry.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Jackery solar generator should I buy in 2026?

For most people, the Explorer 1000 Plus ($619) is the best Jackery: 1,264Wh, 2,000W output, and 800W solar input. For expandable home backup, the HomePower 2000 Plus v2 ($1,069) starts at 2,048Wh and expands to 12kWh. For camping and electronics, the Explorer 300D ($209) weighs 5.5 lbs but has no AC outlets. The Explorer 1000 v2 ($559) is the lighter 1kWh option, and the Explorer 2000 v2 ($899) is the cheapest 2kWh Jackery.

Are Jackery power stations LiFePO4?

Yes. Every model in this guide uses LiFePO4 (lithium iron phosphate) cells. The Explorer 1000 Plus, 300D, 1000 v2, and 2000 v2 are rated for 4,000 cycles, and the HomePower 2000 Plus v2 is rated for 6,000+ cycles.

Can I still expand the Jackery Explorer 1000 Plus?

Not with new batteries. Jackery has discontinued the Battery Pack 1000 Plus, so the Explorer 1000 Plus is best treated as a fixed 1,264Wh station. If you want an expandable system, the HomePower 2000 Plus v2 expands from 2,048Wh to 12kWh.

What happened to the Jackery Explorer 300 Plus, 600 Plus, and 2000 Plus?

As of October 2026, Jackery's U.S. store no longer sells the Explorer 300 Plus or 600 Plus as standalone units, and the Explorer 2000 Plus is only offered in kits or refurbished. Their closest current replacements are the Explorer 300D, Explorer 1000 v2, and HomePower 2000 Plus v2.

Does the Jackery Explorer 300D have AC outlets?

No. The Explorer 300D is DC-only: it outputs up to 300W through USB-C, USB-A, and DC ports, including a built-in 140W USB-C cable. It suits phones, laptops, cameras, lights, and Starlink Mini, but not appliances with standard wall plugs.
