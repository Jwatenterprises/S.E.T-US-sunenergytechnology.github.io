This universal compatibility is one of the biggest advantages BougeRV panels have over brand-specific panels — you’re not locked into one ecosystem.

12V Portable Fridges

❄️ 12V Fridges

If you’ve been using an ice cooler on camping trips, a 12V compressor fridge is the single biggest quality-of-life upgrade you can make. No melted ice, no soggy food, no $10 gas station ice runs every morning. BougeRV’s 12V fridges are compressor-based — they actually freeze food down to −4°F, unlike thermoelectric coolers that can only cool 20–30 degrees below ambient.

If you’re building a DIY solar system — for an RV, van, cabin, or battery bank — BougeRV makes the supporting hardware you need. These aren’t glamorous products, but they’re the difference between a clean, safe installation and a fire hazard held together with zip ties.

If you’re charging a battery bank (not a portable power station), you need a charge controller between your solar panels and your batteries. The Sunflow MPPT is BougeRV’s in-house controller and a solid mid-range choice.

MPPT vs. PWM: MPPT (Maximum Power Point Tracking) is 20–30% more efficient than cheaper PWM controllers. In real terms, a 200W panel paired with an MPPT controller delivers closer to 180–190W of actual charging power, while the same panel with a PWM controller might deliver only 140–150W. Over the life of a solar system, the MPPT controller pays for itself in recovered energy.

The Sunflow handles up to 400W of solar input on 12V systems (or 800W on 24V), supports LiFePO4, AGM, gel, and lead-acid batteries, and includes low-temperature charging protection to prevent lithium battery damage in cold weather. The LCD display shows real-time charging current, voltage, and daily energy totals.

Mounting Hardware & Wiring

BougeRV sells the individual components for DIY solar installations. Here’s what each piece does and whether you need it:

  • Z-Mount Brackets ($25/set) — Bolt rigid panels to RV roofs, cabin roofs, or ground frames. 4 brackets per set. You need these for any permanent rigid panel installation.
  • Adjustable Tilt Mount ($45) — Portable ground mount with adjustable angle legs. Sets a rigid panel at the optimal sun angle without a permanent installation. Great for off-grid cabins or seasonal use.
  • MC4 Extension Cable ($15) — Extends the run from panel to controller/station. Standard 10AWG, 20ft length. You need at least one if your panels are more than 6ft from your battery or power station.
  • MC4 Crimping Tool Kit ($29) — Professional-grade crimping pliers + MC4 connectors for making custom-length cables. Essential for DIY builders. Overkill if you’re just plugging into a power station.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is BougeRV a good brand for solar panels?

Yes. BougeRV has sold 400,000+ solar products with a 4.5+ star average on Amazon. Their 200W portable panel ($199) is one of the best-selling portable panels in the overlanding market. Monocrystalline PERC cells with ETFE coating deliver 21–23% efficiency — competitive with premium brand-specific panels from EcoFlow and Jackery at a lower price.

Can I use BougeRV panels with EcoFlow, Jackery, or BLUETTI?

Yes. BougeRV panels use standard MC4 connectors. BLUETTI and ALLPOWERS accept MC4 natively. EcoFlow needs an MC4 to XT60 adapter ($8–12). Jackery needs an MC4 to Anderson adapter ($8–12). BougeRV panels are among the most universally compatible panels on the market.

BougeRV 200W vs Renogy 200W — which should I buy?

For portable ground use at campsites, BougeRV ($199, bifold, kickstand, lighter). For permanent roof-mount on an RV or cabin, Renogy (rigid aluminum frame, tempered glass, longer track record in permanent installations). Both deliver similar real-world output.

Is the BougeRV 12V fridge worth it for camping?

Absolutely. It replaces ice coolers entirely. The 53Qt ASPEN ($299) is a compressor-based fridge that cools to −4°F, runs on 12V DC or 110V AC, draws ~45W actively, and holds temp for hours on eco mode. For half the price of a Dometic, you get 90% of the performance.

What accessories do I need for a DIY solar setup?

For a battery bank system: Sunflow 30A MPPT Controller ($89) + MC4 Extension Cable ($15) + mounting hardware (Z-brackets $25 or tilt mount $45). For a portable power station: just the panel and possibly an MC4 adapter for your station’s input connector — no controller needed.
