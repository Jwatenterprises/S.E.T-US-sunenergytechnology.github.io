OSCAL PowerMax 3600 SE vs BLUETTI AC200L vs EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max — Mid-Range Showdown

Update, Oct 31, 2026: We rewrote this comparison for the OSCAL PowerMax 3600 SE. It replaced the PowerMax 3600 we covered in September at $699, and it sells for $1,199, so the verdict below has changed. All prices are from each brand’s US store as of October 31, 2026.

Three LiFePO4 solar generators between $899 and $1,199. The cheapest is the best fit for most homes; the biggest has 76% more capacity for $300 more. Here’s the full breakdown.

By S.E.T. Editorial Team Updated October 31, 2026 13 min read
Quick answer: For most homes, the BLUETTI AC200L ($899) is the best value: 2,048Wh and 2,400W for the lowest price here, with expansion batteries when you need more. The OSCAL PowerMax 3600 SE ($1,199) gives you the most capacity per dollar — 3,600Wh, 3,600W and 2,400W of solar input at $0.33/Wh — but it weighs 98 lbs. The EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max ($1,049) wins on portability (51 lbs) and wall charging (0–80% in 43 minutes). All three use LiFePO4 batteries with 3,000+ cycles. Prices from each brand’s US store as of October 31, 2026.

Why These Three?

The mid-range solar generator market sits between about $900 and $1,200. That’s where most first-time buyers land — enough power for real backup (fridge, lights, router, laptops) without the $2,000+ commitment of a whole-home system.

These three units define the tier. They share the same battery chemistry (LiFePO4), similar port layouts, and a price tag that puts them head-to-head. But the differences are significant enough to matter.

Let’s break them down one at a time, then stack them side by side.

Ports: 14 outlets in total, including 2x USB-C (100W), 4x USB-A and an Anderson DC port.

The honest take: OSCAL is a newer brand in portable power, without the review library of BLUETTI or EcoFlow. The capacity advantage is real, but at $1,199 it’s no longer half the price of the others. Pick it if you need 3.6kWh in one unit or lots of solar input; if 2kWh covers you, the AC200L does the job for $300 less.

🏆 Best Value for Most Homes: BLUETTI AC200L

At $899, the AC200L is the lowest price here for the same 2,048Wh and 2,400W as the DELTA 2 Max, and it can grow with expansion batteries. For a fridge, lights, router and laptops through an overnight outage, it covers you for the least money.

🔋 Most Capacity per Dollar: OSCAL PowerMax 3600 SE

At $0.33/Wh, the OSCAL still delivers the most energy per dollar: 3,600Wh for $1,199, against $0.44/Wh for the AC200L and $0.51/Wh for the DELTA 2 Max. The gap is smaller than it was with the old $699 PowerMax 3600, and you pay for it in weight (98 lbs).

🔌 Best Ecosystem: BLUETTI AC200L

BLUETTI’s expansion battery lineup is the deepest of these three brands. The B210, B300, and B300S give you flexible growth paths without replacing your base unit. Add a community with millions of units in the field, and you get the safest “bet on the brand” option in this tier.

💫 Best Portability: EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max

At 51 lbs, the EcoFlow is 11 lbs lighter than the BLUETTI and 47 lbs lighter than the OSCAL. For RV users, campers, and anyone who moves their generator regularly, that weight difference matters every single time you pick it up.

☀️ Best Solar Charging: OSCAL PowerMax 3600 SE

2,400W of solar input is more than double the EcoFlow’s 1,000W and double the BLUETTI’s 1,200W. Off-grid users who depend on solar panels for recharging will fill the OSCAL fastest — critical during extended outages or remote camping.

⚡ Best Fast Charging: EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max

X-Stream technology delivers 0–80% in 43 minutes from a wall outlet. When a storm warning drops and you need a full battery fast, EcoFlow’s charging speed is unmatched. The other two take roughly 2 hours for a full wall charge.

Who Should Buy Which?

Buy the OSCAL PowerMax 3600 SE if: You need 3.6kWh in a single unit and want the lowest cost per watt-hour. You’re comfortable with a newer brand. You need high solar input for off-grid use. It will stay in one place (98 lbs). You want to run power-hungry devices (3,600W continuous).
Buy the BLUETTI AC200L if: You want solid home backup for the lowest price ($899). Brand trust matters to you. You want the option to expand capacity over time with B300 batteries. You value the wireless charging pad. You’re investing in a system you’ll build on for years.
Buy the EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max if: You move your generator regularly (RV, van, between home and campsite). You want the fastest wall charging in the industry. Weight is a deciding factor. You want X-Boost to handle high-wattage devices beyond the rated 2,400W.
Don’t overspend on capacity you won’t use. If you only need backup for phones, a laptop, and lights (under 500Wh/day), all three of these units are overkill. Check our budget solar generator guide for units under $300 that cover lightweight use cases.

The Bottom Line

This comparison looks different than it did in September. The old PowerMax 3600 at $699 was a runaway value; its replacement, the PowerMax 3600 SE, is $1,199. It still has the biggest numbers — capacity, output, solar input — and the lowest cost per watt-hour ($0.33 vs. $0.44–$0.51), but it’s now the most expensive of the three up front, and the heaviest by far.

For most homes, the BLUETTI AC200L at $899 is the smarter buy: the lowest price, a proven brand, and expansion batteries when you need more. Choose the EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max ($1,049) if you move your unit a lot or need the fastest wall charging.

Choose the OSCAL PowerMax 3600 SE if you need 3.6kWh in one box and plenty of solar input, and you’re fine with a newer brand and a 98 lb unit that stays put.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the OSCAL PowerMax 3600 SE worth buying over BLUETTI or EcoFlow?

Only if you need the extra capacity. At $1,199 it has the lowest cost per watt-hour ($0.33) and 3,600Wh in one unit, but it costs $300 more than the BLUETTI AC200L ($899), weighs 98 lbs, and OSCAL is a newer brand. If 2kWh covers your needs, the AC200L is the better value.

What is the best mid-range solar generator in 2026?

Best value for most homes: BLUETTI AC200L ($899, 2,048Wh, expandable). Most capacity per dollar: OSCAL PowerMax 3600 SE ($1,199, 3,600Wh). Best portability: EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max ($1,049, 51 lbs, 43-min fast charge). All three use LiFePO4 with 3,000+ cycle life. Prices as of October 31, 2026.

How much solar panel wattage do these generators support?

OSCAL: 2,400W (highest — OSCAL rates a full recharge at about 2.25 hours in ideal sun). BLUETTI: 1,200W (~2.5–3 hours). EcoFlow: 1,000W (~3–4 hours). Higher solar input means faster off-grid recharging, which matters most during extended outages and remote camping.

Can these solar generators power a whole house?

Essential circuits, yes — fridge, lights, Wi-Fi, phone chargers, laptops. Not a full house with central HVAC. The OSCAL at 3,600Wh runs essentials for 24–36 hours. The BLUETTI and EcoFlow at 2,048Wh cover 14–20 hours, and both take expansion batteries for longer backup. For whole-home HVAC backup, look at the EcoFlow DELTA Pro Ultra or BLUETTI Apex 300 class.
