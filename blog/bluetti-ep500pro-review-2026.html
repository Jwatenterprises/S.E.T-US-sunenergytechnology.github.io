What It Can Actually Power

The EP500Pro's 3,000W continuous output handles the vast majority of household loads. But unlike the EcoFlow DELTA Pro Ultra (7,200W), it can't run central air conditioning or large electric dryers. Here's the honest breakdown.

Loads It Handles

  • Full refrigerator/freezer (150-400W running) — runs for 12+ hours on a single charge
  • Window AC unit (500-1,500W) — the 6,000W surge absorbs startup; runs for 3-10 hours depending on BTU
  • CPAP machine (30-60W) — runs all night, every night, for weeks between charges
  • Wi-Fi router + modem (15-30W) — over 100 hours of runtime
  • Multiple TVs, laptops, phone chargers, LED lights — all day, no problem
  • Microwave (1,000-1,500W) — no issue for cooking cycles
  • Sump pump (800-1,500W running) — critical for basements during storm outages
  • Home office (computer, monitor, router, printer) — 8+ hours easily

Loads It Can't Handle

  • Central air conditioning (3,000-5,000W running, 6,000-10,000W startup) — exceeds the 3,000W continuous limit
  • Electric dryer (2,400-5,400W, requires 240V) — needs Fusion Box Pro + exceeds output for most dryers
  • Well pump (1,000-2,000W running, but requires 240V in most setups) — needs Fusion Box Pro
  • EV charger (Level 2 requires 240V and 3,600W+) — beyond EP500Pro's rated output

The Killer Feature: Built-In Wheels

No other home battery has this. Tesla Powerwall bolts to your wall. EcoFlow DELTA Pro Ultra sits on casters you can add, but at 187 lbs it's not designed to be moved regularly. FranklinWH aPower 2 is hardwired into your breaker panel.

The EP500Pro rolls. Four built-in caster wheels, two of them locking. At 179 lbs, you're not carrying it — but you can push it from the garage to the living room, roll it onto a truck during an evacuation, or move it between a primary home and a cabin.

This matters most for:

  • Hurricane zones (Florida, Texas Gulf, Carolinas) — when evacuation orders hit, your backup power leaves with you
  • Wildfire-prone areas (California, Pacific Northwest) — same logic, different disaster
  • Renters — take it with you when you move
  • Seasonal use — primary home in winter, lake house in summer

EP500Pro vs. EcoFlow DELTA Pro Ultra

The DELTA Pro Ultra (full review) wins on raw power: 7,200W output runs central AC, dryers, and EV chargers. It also has built-in 240V and 5,600W solar input. If you need to power heavy 240V loads, the DELTA Pro Ultra is the better machine at a lower price ($3,999 vs $5,999).

The EP500Pro wins on longevity (3,500+ cycles vs 3,000+), mobility (built-in wheels vs static unit), UPS mode (built-in <20ms switchover vs requiring Smart Home Panel), and time-of-use programming (daily cycling to save on electricity bills). If you don't need central AC and value the ability to physically move your home battery, the EP500Pro is the smarter buy.

EP500Pro vs. Tesla Powerwall

Tesla Powerwall dominates on output (11.5kW), capacity (13.5kWh), seamless grid integration, and warranty (10 years). But it costs $9,200+ installed, requires a certified installer, and has a 3-6 month waitlist in most markets. It's permanently wall-mounted — it's not going anywhere.

The EP500Pro costs $3,200 less, ships in days, requires no installer, has superior cycle life (3,500+ vs ~2,000), and — critically — you can take it with you. For homeowners who want backup power now without the installer bottleneck, the EP500Pro delivers.

The bottom line on competition: Need to run central AC? Get the DELTA Pro Ultra. Want the absolute best grid integration and don't mind waiting? Tesla Powerwall. Want the longest battery life, built-in portability, and plug-and-play simplicity? The EP500Pro is your machine.

Solar Charging — 2,400W Input

The EP500Pro accepts up to 2,400W of solar input via its dual MPPT charge controller. In practical terms:

  • 4 × 200W panels (800W) recharges the 5.1kWh battery in about 6-7 hours of peak sun.
  • 6 × 400W panels (2,400W) cuts that to about 2.5 hours.
  • With B300 expansion (11.3kWh total), the full 2,400W solar input recharges the entire system in about 5 hours of peak sun.

This isn't as fast as the DELTA Pro Ultra's 5,600W solar input, but it's more than enough for most residential setups. With a 1,200W+ solar array and sunny conditions, you can keep essential loads running indefinitely during extended outages. Time-of-Use Programming

This is a feature most portable power stations and even the DELTA Pro Ultra lack. The BLUETTI App lets you program the EP500Pro to charge from the grid during off-peak hours (typically overnight) and discharge during peak pricing hours (typically 4-9 PM). Over a year, this can save $200-600 on electricity bills depending on your rate structure.

Combined with solar panels, the EP500Pro can function as a daily-cycling home battery — similar to how Tesla Powerwall operates. Charge from solar during the day, use stored power during peak evening hours, top off from the grid overnight at cheap rates. The 3,500+ cycle life means the battery is built for this kind of daily use for 10+ years.

Is the BLUETTI EP500Pro worth $5,999?
