What Makes the TK2 Stand Out

1. It Actually Folds

A lot of “folding” e-bikes barely shrink. The TK2 folds down to 39.4 × 28.7 × 26.8 inches — compact enough for a car trunk, RV storage bay, or apartment closet. The folding mechanism has been tested for 100,000+ cycles, and hidden cable routing means nothing snags or kinks when you fold.

2. Reverse Mode

This is genuinely useful. Hit reverse on the controller and the TK2 backs up at 2.5 MPH. In tight driveways, parking lots, or when you overshoot a parking spot, you don’t have to awkwardly drag 90 lbs of trike backwards. This is a feature most e-trikes skip, and it makes the TK2 dramatically easier to live with.

3. Rear Differential

Most budget trikes have a solid rear axle — both wheels turn at the same speed, which makes corners feel unstable and tippy. The TK2 has a forged alloy rear differential that lets each rear wheel spin independently. This is the same principle that makes cars corner smoothly. It makes the TK2 feel planted and predictable in turns.

4. Hydraulic Disc Brakes with E-Cutoff

Mechanical disc brakes require more hand pressure and wear faster. The TK2 uses hydraulic disc brakes with 180mm rotors front and rear. They stop hard with minimal hand effort — important for riders with arthritis or limited grip strength. The e-cutoff instantly kills motor power when you pull either brake lever.

5. 3.5″ Color TFT Display (1,500 nits)

Most e-trikes have a cheap LCD that washes out in sunlight. The TK2 has a full-color TFT display at 1,500 nits with anti-reflective coating. It shows speed, battery percentage, range estimate, assist level, and trip data — all visible in direct sun. IPX5 water resistant.

Solar Charging the TK2

This is where the S.E.T. Solar angle comes in. The TK2’s battery is 720Wh (48V × 15Ah). Here’s the math:

  • From a wall outlet: 120W draw × 6–8 hours = full charge for about $0.09 (at national average $0.12/kWh)
  • From a solar generator: Any unit with 200W+ AC output handles it. A ALLPOWERS R3500 (3,168Wh) can charge the TK2 over 4 times. A EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max (2,048Wh) covers almost 3 full charges.
  • From solar panels: A 400W panel setup produces ~2,000Wh on a good sun day. That’s nearly 3 full TK2 charges per day — completely free.

If you ride 10–15 miles daily and charge with solar, your transportation cost drops to $0. No gas, no insurance, no registration. That’s the off-grid mobility loop.
