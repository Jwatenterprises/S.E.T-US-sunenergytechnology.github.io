Why "Systems You Can Actually Buy" Matters

The home battery market has a dirty secret: the most-talked-about products are the hardest to actually get.

Tesla Powerwall requires a Tesla-certified installer, often comes bundled with a Tesla solar roof or panel purchase, and wait times stretch 3–6 months depending on your area. You can't order one on Amazon. You can't install it yourself. You're at Tesla's mercy for scheduling.

Enphase IQ Battery is sold exclusively through authorized solar distributors and installers. No Amazon listing. No direct consumer purchase. No DIY installation. You need a certified Enphase installer to buy one, mount it, and commission it.

Meanwhile, millions of homeowners just want a battery they can order today, receive this week, and plug in themselves. A system that charges from the wall when the grid is up and keeps the fridge, lights, and internet running when it isn't. Something that doesn't require a $15,000 installed solar system just to get backup power.

That's this guide. Every system here ships direct, works out of the box, and scales from a single unit in an apartment to a whole-home backup installation. No installer waitlists. No bundled solar requirements. No gatekeeping.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I install a home battery backup myself?

Yes, for all five systems in this guide. They work as plug-and-play units out of the box — charge from a wall outlet, then plug your devices directly into the battery during outages. For whole-home integration (automatic switchover when the grid drops), the EcoFlow Smart Home Panel requires a licensed electrician for the panel install, but the battery units themselves need no special wiring or permits. This is the key advantage over Tesla Powerwall and Enphase, which require certified installers for the entire system.

How long will a home battery power my house during an outage?

At a typical essential load of 600W (fridge, lights, router, phones, CPAP): a 3.6kWh system runs for ~6 hours, a 5.1kWh system for ~8.5 hours, a 6.1kWh system for ~10 hours, and a fully expanded 25kWh system for 40+ hours. With solar panels recharging the battery during the day, a properly sized system can run essentials indefinitely. For whole-home backup including air conditioning, you need 15–25kWh minimum.

Do I need solar panels for a home battery to work?

No. All five systems charge from a standard wall outlet. Solar panels extend your autonomy during multi-day outages by recharging the battery during daylight, but they're optional. Many homeowners start with battery-only and add solar panels later. When you do add panels, every system on this list has built-in MPPT solar charge controllers ready to connect.

Do home batteries qualify for the federal tax credit?

Yes. Under the Inflation Reduction Act, standalone energy storage systems qualify for the 30% federal Investment Tax Credit (ITC) starting in 2023. This applies to batteries 3kWh and larger, whether or not they're paired with solar panels. All five systems on this list meet the 3kWh minimum. A $3,999 DELTA Pro Ultra purchase would yield a ~$1,200 tax credit. Consult your tax professional for eligibility based on your specific situation.

What's the difference between these and a portable power station?

Capacity and integration. Portable power stations (like the ones in our home backup guide) typically range from 300–3,200Wh and power individual devices via direct plug-in. Home battery systems start at 3,500Wh, scale to 90,000Wh, and can integrate with your home's electrical panel for automatic whole-home switchover. The Mango Power E and EcoFlow DELTA Pro bridge both categories — they work as portable power stations and home backups.
