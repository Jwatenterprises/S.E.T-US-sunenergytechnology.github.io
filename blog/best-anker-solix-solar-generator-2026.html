How We Ranked the Anker SOLIX Lineup

Anker is better known for phone chargers and cables, but their SOLIX division has quietly built one of the most competitive solar generator lineups on the market. The range spans from a sub-$500 portable unit to a whole-home battery system that competes directly with the Tesla Powerwall — and every model uses LiFePO4 chemistry with fast-charging speeds that embarrass most of the competition.

We ranked these four models on five factors:

  • Value. Capacity, output, and charging speed per dollar — not just sticker price.
  • Performance. Real continuous output, surge handling, and how fast each unit recharges from solar and from the wall.
  • Expandability. Whether you can grow the system later without replacing it.
  • Battery chemistry. Cycle life, thermal stability, and usable capacity.
  • Real-world use. Not "can it technically power X" but "will it comfortably power X for as long as you need it."

Price: ~$469.99 • 0–80% Charge: 43 minutes

The C1000 is our top pick because it does something almost no other power station in its price range can: charge from empty to 80% in 43 minutes. That's not a theoretical number — it's Anker's HyperFlash technology delivering 1,300W of AC charging speed in a 27.8 lb package. When a storm warning drops and you have an hour to prep, that speed matters.

At 1,056Wh and 1,800W continuous output, the C1000 comfortably runs a full-size refrigerator for 8–10 hours, a CPAP machine all night, or a laptop and router setup for 24+ hours. It accepts up to 600W of solar input, so a pair of 200W panels can top it off in about 3.5 hours of direct sun.

For a camping trip, emergency kit, or apartment-dweller's backup plan, the C1000 hits a sweet spot that most competitors miss: it's light enough to carry, fast enough to charge on short notice, and powerful enough to actually run a fridge.

When you need to run a refrigerator, a window AC unit, and your router all at the same time, the F3000 is the unit that actually does it without tripping. 3,072Wh of LiFePO4 capacity and 3,600W of continuous output (6,000W surge) put it in direct competition with the EcoFlow DELTA Pro — and it wins on solar input with 2,400W versus EcoFlow's 1,600W.

For a homeowner in hurricane country or anyone who needs multi-day outage coverage, the F3000 can run your essential circuits for 24–48 hours depending on load, and recharge from solar in just over an hour with a high-wattage panel array. It's not portable in the way the C1000 is — at 55.12 lbs, it's meant to live in the garage or basement — but it packs genuine home-backup capability into a single unit.

The C2000 Gen 2 replaces the F2600 as our mid-range pick. The F2600 now lists at $1,999.99 on ankersolix.com — more than the larger F3000 — so it no longer makes sense as a value buy.

The C2000 Gen 2 gives you 2,048Wh of LiFePO4 capacity and 2,400W of output in a 15.3 kg (about 33.7 lb) unit. Solar input reaches 800W for a full charge in about three hours, and Anker rates the battery for 4,000 cycles.

The E10 is a different category entirely. This isn't a portable power station — it's a permanent whole-home battery system that competes directly with the Tesla Powerwall and Enphase IQ. Starting at 6kWh and expandable to a staggering 90kWh, the E10 connects directly to your home's electrical panel and provides seamless grid switchover. When the power goes out, your lights don't even flicker.

The E10 competes with the Tesla Powerwall while offering more flexible scaling — check Anker’s site for current installed pricing. It integrates with rooftop solar panels for true energy independence, and the 6,000+ cycle LiFePO4 battery means a 15–20 year lifespan with daily cycling. For homeowners in hurricane zones, wildfire areas, or anywhere with unreliable grid power, the E10 is the "set it and forget it" solution.

This ranks #4 not because it's worse — it's arguably the most capable product on this list — but because its whole-home price and professional installation requirement put it in a different buying decision than portable power stations. If you're comparing it to a gas generator or a Tesla Powerwall, it wins. If you're comparing it to a C1000 for camping, they're different tools for different jobs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Anker SOLIX should I buy in 2026?

For most people, the C1000 ($469.99) is the best Anker SOLIX: 1,056Wh, 1,800W output, and 600W solar input. The F3000 ($1,399.99) is the home-backup pick with 3,072Wh and expansion to 24kWh, the C2000 Gen 2 ($899.99) is the best mid-range value, and the E10 is Anker’s whole-home system. Prices are from ankersolix.com on October 1, 2026.

Why is the Anker SOLIX F2600 no longer a top pick?

The F2600 lists at $1,999.99 on ankersolix.com as of October 2026, which is more than the larger F3000 ($1,399.99). The C2000 Gen 2 offers 2,048Wh and 2,400W for $899.99.

Are Anker SOLIX power stations LiFePO4?

Yes. The C1000, C2000 Gen 2, F3000, and E10 all use LiFePO4 cells. The C1000 is rated for 3,000 cycles and the C2000 Gen 2 and F3000 for 4,000 cycles.

Can I expand Anker SOLIX power stations?

Yes. The C1000 accepts a 1,056Wh expansion battery, the C2000 Gen 2 accepts the BP2000 (Gen 2), and the F3000 expands to 24kWh with BP3000 batteries.

How fast does the Anker SOLIX C1000 recharge?

The C1000 accepts up to 1,300W of AC input and 600W of solar. Anker’s newer C1000 Gen 2 recharges even faster if charging speed is your priority.
