Tesla Powerwall 3 vs FranklinWH aPower 2: Which Home Battery Actually Wins?

The two heavyweights of residential battery storage, compared spec-by-spec. We break down capacity, output, installation, warranty, expandability, and real-world value to pick a winner for 2026.

S.E.T. Editorial Published: September 17, 2026 Read: 12 min

Quick Answer: The Tesla Powerwall 3 wins for new solar installations — its built-in hybrid inverter eliminates a separate component, saves $2,000–$3,000 on total system cost, and delivers 11.5 kW of continuous output at the lowest installed price ($13,000–$16,500). The FranklinWH aPower 2 wins for retrofits and existing solar systems — it's AC-coupled (works with any inverter), has 15 kWh capacity (11% more), 15 kW surge power (enough to start a 5-ton AC), automatic generator integration, and a 15-year warranty vs Tesla's 10. Both use LiFePO4. Neither qualifies for the federal tax credit in 2026.

New Install vs Retrofit: That's Your Decision

If you're building a solar + battery system from scratch, the Tesla Powerwall 3 is your pick. Its built-in hybrid inverter handles power conversion for both your solar panels and the battery in one box. That means one less piece of equipment, simpler wiring, fewer failure points, and $2,000–$3,000 less in total system cost versus buying a separate inverter.

If you already have solar panels and an inverter on your roof, the FranklinWH aPower 2 is your pick. It's AC-coupled, which means it works with any brand of existing solar inverter — SolarEdge, Enphase, SMA, Fronius, you name it. The aGate controller sits between your solar, battery, grid, and optional generator, managing all four energy sources intelligently. No need to rip out your existing inverter.

Both are permanently installed, professionally wired LiFePO4 systems. Both provide whole-home backup. The question is which scenario describes your home.

Capacity and Power: Different Strengths

The FranklinWH aPower 2 stores 15 kWh per unit — 11% more energy than the Powerwall 3's 13.5 kWh. In a real outage, that's roughly 2–3 extra hours of essential-load runtime (fridge, lights, router, phone charging at ~500W average).

But the Powerwall 3 pushes more power out at once: 11.5 kW continuous versus the aPower 2's 10 kW. That higher continuous output means the Powerwall handles more simultaneous loads without breaking a sweat — important if you're running an electric dryer, an oven, and a heat pump at the same time.

Where FranklinWH flips the script is surge power. The aPower 2 delivers 15 kW for up to 10 seconds — enough to cold-start a 5-ton central air conditioner. The Powerwall 3 has no separate surge rating above its 11.5 kW continuous. If your central AC has a high startup draw, the aPower 2 handles it more reliably.

The aPower 2 is also more efficient: 96% round-trip efficiency vs the Powerwall's 89%. That means for every 100 kWh of solar energy you store, you get 96 kWh back from FranklinWH and only 89 kWh back from Tesla. Over years of daily cycling, that 7-point gap adds up.

Installation and Integration: The Make-or-Break Factor

Tesla Powerwall 3: Built for New Installs

The Powerwall 3's integrated hybrid inverter is its biggest differentiator. It replaces both the battery and the solar inverter in a new installation. Your solar panels connect directly to the Powerwall, which handles DC-to-AC conversion, battery management, grid interaction, and backup switching in one unit.

For a new solar + storage system, this simplifies everything:

  • One device instead of two (no separate solar inverter)
  • $2,000–$3,000 saved on the inverter
  • Simpler wiring, fewer failure points
  • Tesla app controls the entire system

The trade-off: if you already have a solar inverter you're happy with, the Powerwall 3's built-in inverter is redundant. You'd be paying for an inverter you don't need.

FranklinWH aPower 2: Built for Retrofits

The aPower 2 is AC-coupled, meaning it converts energy after your existing inverter has already processed it. This design works with any brand of solar inverter — SolarEdge, Enphase, SMA, Fronius, or any other. You keep your existing solar setup untouched and just add battery storage.

The aGate controller is the aPower 2's command center. It installs at your main electrical panel and manages all energy flows:

  • Solar, battery, grid, and generator — all four sources managed automatically
  • Circuit-level load prioritization via the FranklinWH app
  • Automatic load shedding during outages (drops non-essential circuits first)
  • Generator auto-start/stop when battery runs low

That generator integration is something Tesla simply doesn't offer. If you have a backup generator for extended outages, the aGate can automatically start it when the battery drops below a threshold, recharge the battery, then shut the generator off. It's a fully automated hybrid power system.

Expandability: FranklinWH Wins by a Mile

A single aGate controller supports up to 15 aPower 2 batteries — that's 225 kWh of total storage. Tesla allows up to 4 Powerwall 3 units per home for a maximum of 54 kWh.

For most single-family homes, one or two units of either system is plenty. The difference matters for:

  • Large homes with high energy consumption (4,000+ sq ft, multiple AC zones)
  • Properties aiming for multi-day off-grid autonomy
  • Small commercial or agricultural applications
  • EV owners who want to charge from stored solar overnight

If you think you'll ever need more than 54 kWh, FranklinWH is your only option here.

Warranty and Longevity

FranklinWH offers a 15-year warranty on the aPower 2. Tesla offers 10 years with a 70% capacity retention guarantee. That's a significant gap — 5 extra years of coverage means the aPower 2 is warranted until 2041 if you install it today, versus 2036 for the Powerwall.

Both use LiFePO4 chemistry, which is inherently more stable, safer, and longer-lasting than older lithium-ion formulations. Real-world LiFePO4 batteries typically last 15–20+ years before reaching 80% capacity, so both systems will likely outlast their warranties.

That said, FranklinWH's longer warranty provides more peace of mind, especially given the installed cost. A $15,000–$20,000 system with only a 10-year warranty is a harder sell than one with 15 years of coverage.

Price and Value

Tesla wins on price. The Powerwall 3 installs for $13,000–$16,500, while the aPower 2 (with the required aGate controller) runs $15,000–$20,000. At the low end, that's a $2,000 difference. At the high end, it's $3,500.

On a per-kWh basis, Tesla is also cheaper: roughly $963–$1,222 per kWh installed versus $1,000–$1,333 per kWh for FranklinWH.

However, value depends on your situation:

  • New solar install: Tesla's value is even better because the built-in inverter saves $2,000–$3,000 on a component you'd otherwise buy separately.
  • Retrofit: FranklinWH's value is better because you don't need a new inverter. The aPower 2 works with what you have.
  • Generator owners: FranklinWH's automatic generator integration adds value no Tesla product can match.
  • Long-term: FranklinWH's 15-year warranty and 96% efficiency deliver better lifetime economics despite the higher upfront cost.

Which One Should You Buy?

Buy the Tesla Powerwall 3 if:

  • You're installing a brand-new solar + battery system from scratch
  • You want the simplest, most streamlined installation with one integrated device
  • Budget is a priority and you want the lowest installed cost
  • You don't own a backup generator (and don't plan to)
  • You need maximum continuous output (11.5 kW) for high-draw loads

Buy the FranklinWH aPower 2 if:

  • You already have solar panels and an inverter installed
  • You want to keep your existing solar setup and just add battery storage
  • You own a backup generator and want automatic start/stop integration
  • You need to start a large central AC unit (15 kW surge)
  • Long-term warranty matters to you (15 years vs 10)
  • You may expand beyond 54 kWh in the future

Our Bottom Line Verdict

For homeowners building a new solar + storage system in 2026, the Tesla Powerwall 3 is the better value. The integrated inverter simplifies installation, reduces cost, and delivers the highest continuous output. Tesla's ecosystem is polished and the installed price is $2,000–$3,500 less than FranklinWH.

For homeowners adding battery storage to an existing solar system, the FranklinWH aPower 2 is the smarter choice. AC-coupling means zero disruption to your current setup, the aGate controller provides intelligent energy management with generator auto-start, and the 15-year warranty with 96% efficiency delivers better lifetime economics.

Our pick for new installs: Tesla Powerwall 3.
Our pick for retrofits: FranklinWH aPower 2.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Tesla Powerwall 3 or FranklinWH aPower 2 better for home backup?

It depends on your situation. The Tesla Powerwall 3 is best for new solar installations — its built-in hybrid inverter eliminates a separate component and saves $2,000–$3,000. The FranklinWH aPower 2 is best for retrofits — it works with any existing inverter, offers a longer 15-year warranty, 15 kW surge (starts a 5-ton AC), and automatic generator integration that Tesla doesn't support.

How much do they cost installed?

Tesla Powerwall 3: $13,000–$16,500 fully installed. FranklinWH aPower 2: $15,000–$20,000 fully installed (including the required aGate controller). The 30% federal tax credit expired December 31, 2025. Some state incentives may still apply.

Does the federal tax credit still apply to home batteries in 2026?

No. The 30% federal residential clean energy credit (IRC Section 25D) expired December 31, 2025 under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. Neither battery qualifies for the federal credit in 2026. Check your state's DSIRE database for state-level incentives that may still be available.

Can I install either battery myself?

No. Both require professional installation by certified electricians with permits and inspections. Tesla requires a Tesla-certified installer; FranklinWH requires a FranklinWH-certified installer. If you want a home backup you can set up yourself, consider plug-and-play alternatives like the EcoFlow DELTA Pro Ultra, BLUETTI EP500Pro, or Mango Power E.

Which is more expandable?

FranklinWH by a wide margin. A single aGate supports up to 15 aPower 2 units for 225 kWh total. Tesla allows up to 4 Powerwalls for 54 kWh. For most homes, either is sufficient, but large properties or small commercial applications need FranklinWH's headroom.
