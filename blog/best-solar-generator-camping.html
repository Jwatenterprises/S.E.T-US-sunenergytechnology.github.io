Camping Power Is a Different Game

Most "best solar generator" lists rank by capacity and output. That makes sense for home backup. But camping? Camping is about tradeoffs that home backup doesn't care about.

Can you carry it from the car to the campsite without blowing out your back? Will it recharge from a solar panel while you're out hiking? Does it have enough ports to charge your phone, your partner's phone, a Bluetooth speaker, and a set of camp lights simultaneously?

We ranked these 5 picks specifically for camping — where weight, portability, solar recharge speed, and port availability matter more than peak wattage. Every unit here is something we'd actually take on a trip.

What to Look for in a Camping Solar Generator

Camping power stations live in a different world than home backup units. Here's what actually matters when you're sleeping in a tent.

Weight-to-Capacity Ratio

This is the single most important camping metric. Divide the capacity (Wh) by weight (lbs) and you get Wh-per-pound — how much energy you carry per unit of effort.

  • Jackery Explorer 1000 Pro: 39.4 Wh/lb — best on this list
  • ALLPOWERS R600: 37.8 Wh/lb — strong budget efficiency
  • Jackery Explorer 300 Plus: 37.4 Wh/lb
  • EcoFlow River 3 Plus: 37.1 Wh/lb
  • BLUETTI AC2A: 32.9 Wh/lb — lowest, but also lightest overall

If you're car camping and weight doesn't matter much, the Jackery 1000 Pro gives you the most energy per pound. If you're carrying it on your back, absolute weight matters more than the ratio — go with the AC2A at 6.2 lbs.

Solar Recharge Speed

When you're off-grid for multiple days, solar recharge is your lifeline. The faster your unit recharges, the less you worry about rationing power.

Pro tip: Solar recharge times are measured in "ideal conditions" — clear sky, panel perpendicular to the sun, peak hours (10am-2pm). In real camping conditions with partial shade, angled panels, and cloud cover, add 50-100% to the listed recharge time. A "1.8-hour" solar recharge becomes 3-4 hours in practice.

Jackery's SolarSaga ecosystem is the most proven for camping. Their panels fold flat, have built-in kickstands, and are designed to pair with their power stations. EcoFlow and ALLPOWERS have compatible panels too, but the Jackery experience is the most polished for outdoor use.

Number and Type of Ports

At camp, you're charging multiple things simultaneously: phones, headlamps, cameras, speakers, lanterns. Count the ports before you buy:

  • USB-A ports: For older devices, headlamps, camp lights
  • USB-C PD: For laptops, modern phones, drones (the ALLPOWERS R600 and EcoFlow River 3 Plus both have 100W USB-C PD)
  • AC outlets: For anything with a standard plug. Even one AC outlet is useful for a CPAP, small blender, or battery charger
  • 12V/DC: For car accessories, some camp coolers

Durability and Weather Resistance

Camping power stations will get bumped, set in damp grass, exposed to morning dew, and occasionally rained on. None of these units are waterproof (don't submerge them), but some handle the elements better than others. The Jackery units have rubberized corners and are built for outdoor use. The BLUETTI AC2A is well-sealed. The ALLPOWERS R600 is sturdy but less refined in the shell construction.

Always keep your power station off the bare ground. Set it on a dry bag, a flat rock, or inside your vestibule. Morning condensation on cold metal can cause issues over time.

Car Charging Capability

Every unit on this list charges from a 12V car outlet. This turns your drive to the campsite into charging time. A 300Wh unit fully charges in about 2-3 hours via car — meaning it's topped off before you arrive. The Jackery 1000 Pro takes longer (5-6 hours) via 12V, so plan to supplement with solar at camp.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much solar generator capacity do I need for camping?
For most campers, 300-1,000Wh covers everything you'll realistically need. A weekend car camper charging phones, running LED lights, and powering a small fan can get by with 300Wh. If you're running a portable fridge/cooler, charging camera batteries, and powering multiple devices, aim for 500-1,000Wh. Backpackers who only need phone and headlamp charging can use the 200Wh range. The rule of thumb: add up your devices' watt-hours per day, multiply by the number of days without reliable solar recharge, and add a 20% buffer. Our calculator does this math for you.
Can I charge a solar generator while driving?
Yes — every unit on this list supports 12V car charging via the cigarette lighter or DC port. The EcoFlow River 3 Plus and ALLPOWERS R600 charge to full in about 2-3 hours via car. The Jackery Explorer 1000 Pro takes 5-6 hours due to its larger capacity. This makes the drive to your campsite productive charging time. Just make sure your car is running — charging from a parked car's battery can drain it. Some newer vehicles with 12V outlets that stay active when parked have built-in low-voltage cutoffs, but don't count on it.
What can a 1000Wh solar generator power at camp?
A 1,000Wh solar generator like the Jackery Explorer 1000 Pro can power: a portable fridge/cooler (50W draw) for 15-20 hours, a laptop charged 8-10 times, smartphones charged 60+ times, LED camp lights for 80+ hours, a portable fan for 20+ hours, a CPAP machine for 3-4 nights, or a small TV for 8-10 hours. You won't be running a microwave, hair dryer, or electric kettle. The key for multi-day camping is recharging via solar during the day — set panels up at camp in the morning, and you can extend a 1,000Wh unit across an entire week of camping.
