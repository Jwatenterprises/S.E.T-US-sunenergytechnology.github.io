Methodology

How We Score Products

What the S.E.T. Research Score measures, where the numbers come from, and what it is not.

Last updated: November 11, 2026
The Short Version

A research score, not a lab test

The S.E.T. Research Score is our summary of how well a product fits the job it's being recommended for. It is built from published manufacturer specifications, current retailer prices, warranty terms and third-party data. We do not bench-test, lab-test or own every product we score, and we never claim hands-on testing we didn't do.

What Goes Into It

The five factors

  • Capacity & output: usable battery size (Wh), continuous and surge output, and whether that covers the loads in the article (fridge, AC, CPAP, tools).
  • Value: price per watt-hour and what's included at the current retailer price.
  • Charging: AC and solar input limits and the manufacturer's stated charge times.
  • Durability & support: battery chemistry and rated cycle life, warranty length, and the brand's US support presence.
  • Versatility: expandability, outlet mix, UPS/switchover time and weight.

Scores shown out of 5 or out of 100 use the same factors on a different scale. A score compares products within the same article and use case; it isn't an absolute grade across every category.

Sources & Updates

Where the numbers come from

We use each manufacturer's US product page, user manual and FAQ first. When regions list different specs (for example, a UK model with a higher output rating), we use the US figure. Prices are checked on retailer pages and dated in the article, because they change often. When we find a mistake, we fix it and update the page.

Independence

Commissions don't set scores

We earn affiliate commissions on some links (see our affiliate disclosure). A product's commission rate is not one of the five factors, and some of our top picks pay us nothing.
